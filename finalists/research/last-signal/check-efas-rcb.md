- **What we found** (with source URLs)
  - **RCB — mechanism today:**
    - RCB describes Alert RCB as “a new SMS-based system”, used only exceptionally where there is high likelihood of a direct threat to life/health over a significant area; inputs come from ministries, police, fire service, border guard, IMGW and regional authorities, with RCB monitoring 24/7 and activating it. Source: https://www.gov.pl/web/rcb-en/rcb-alert--faq
    - No sign-up, no app, no opt-out: “Anyone in a potentially dangerous area will receive a warning SMS”, operators must send to all users in the area, it “is not part of the RSO app”, and all subscribers receive it “without exception”.
    - Targeting is coarse: “The smallest area for RCB Alerts is a county” [powiat], and operators send it “based on cell tower (BTS) locations”. It is **not** handset-GNSS/Galileo targeting, not street-level, and not cell-broadcast one-to-many radio paging.
    - Handset requirement is minimal: phone “only needs to be on and in range of a mobile network”; prepaid users are included; it is free; RCB “does not collect or process your data—it only sends the alert information to network operators”.
    - Content per official FAQ is only: follow the instructions in the SMS. **Blunt:** the RCB FAQ does **not** specify a 160-character limit, language rules, links, maps, return-periods or evacuation polygons. Do not invent those details on a slide.
  - **RCB — limitation and 2026/27 cell-broadcast claim:**
    - EENA's 14 Oct 2026 update says Poland is “working towards introducing a cell broadcast public warning system by September 2027 to address limitations in its current SMS-based alerts”, that RCB acknowledged sending to millions “can take time” depending on operator capacity, and that cell broadcast would send simultaneously without individual SMS. Source: https://eena.org/newsletter/eena-update-14-10-2026/

Source: https://mapping.emergency.copernicus.eu/news/information-bulletin-173-the-copernicus-emergency-management-service-monitors-floods-in-central-and-eastern-europe/

  - **EFAS/GloFAS data access — September 2024 is in the archive:**
    - Product: **`efas-forecast`** on EWDS, https://ewds.climate.copernicus.eu/datasets/efas-forecast — gridded LISFLOOD output, “1x1 arcminute resolution (~1.5 km at EFAS latitudes)”, initialised “twice daily at 00 and 12 UTC” with 6/24-hour steps and “lead times between 5 and 15 days depending on the forcing”. Formats are GRIB2 and NetCDF-4.
    - Availability: “available from 2018-10-10 up until present with a 30-day delay. The real-time data is only available to EFAS partners”, and temporal coverage is “10 October 2018 to 19 September 2023 and 29 May 2024 to near real-time with a 30-day delay”. **Therefore Sept 2024 runs are public now; Sept 2024 real-time access would not have been.**
    - Version in force for Boris: EFAS **v5.2, released 13 Aug 2024**, so label every Boris forecast “EFAS v5.2”. Its formal-notification criteria were: catchment ≥1,000 km², total probability ≥50% of exceeding the 5-year return period, onset 2–7 days ahead; informal: ≥500 km² and ≥40%. Note the tension: the dataset allows forcing-dependent lead times up to 15 days, EUSPA summarises EFAS as “up to ten days”, and v5.2 formal notifications cap onset at 7 days. Say “10-day forecast product; formal notifications only for onset 2–7 days ahead” — do not blur them.
    - GloFAS is the fallback, not the hero: `cems-glofas-forecast`, 0.05°×0.05° for v4.0, daily — much coarser than EFAS 1 arcmin for Polish catchments. URL: https://ewds.climate.copernicus.eu/datasets/cems-glofas-forecast
    - Archived-data rule: archived EFAS is “open to all, conditional to license conditions acceptance, providing access to all medium-range forecast data after 30 days” via EWDS and MARS; near-real-time (<30 days) is restricted to registered authorised EFAS users via password-protected FTP. Source: https://confluence.ecmwf.int/x/2KdtDg
