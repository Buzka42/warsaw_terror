# ClockWitness — Warsaw technical and commercial review

**Decision cutoff: Saturday, November 28, 2026, at 11:30 CET. Submission: Sunday, November 29, at 15:00 CET; pitches start at 15:05 CET.** The official Warsaw agenda confirms those Sunday times. The decision is not whether you can borrow a receiver. It is whether you can turn a real Galileo capture into a reproducible authentication result, offline, and show why that result changes an operator’s investigation. ([cassini.eu](https://www.cassini.eu/hackathons/poland?utm_source=openai))

**Build ClockWitness, not ChronoShield’s firewall or CERTUS’s compliance autopilot.** One substation timing workflow, one real Galileo recording, one clearly simulated clock trace, one signed evidence package. The winning moment is **“Galileo navigation data authenticated; the delivered clock is still outside tolerance; cause undetermined.”** The action is to open a review ticket and preserve the evidence—not switch a clock, declare an attack, or file a report. This takes ClockWitness’s separation of evidence, ClockEvidence’s negative controls, and the wildcard notes’ strongest idea: sell the handoff between the timing engineer and the SOC. The vote notes do not all endorse the same project, and the ranking’s nine-way convergence is evidence about the ideation process, not a count of Warsaw rivals. ([cassini.eu](https://www.cassini.eu/hackathons/sites/default/files/2026-10/Participant%20Playbook_12th%20CASSINI%20Hackathon.pdf?utm_source=openai))

## 2. What Galileo actually proves—and what the rules engine should say

- **BLOCKER — Do not label a clock, fix or RF signal “Galileo authenticated.”** OSNMA Initial Service was declared operational on **July 24, 2025**. It authenticates selected navigation data carried on Galileo **E1-B I/NAV**, including covered ephemeris/clock and time-related parameters, depending on the data type. The trust chain uses a trusted public key, a signed TESLA root, and delayed key disclosure to validate message tags; it is not a signature on each observation. It does **not** authenticate RF arrival time, pseudorange, the receiver’s mixed-constellation solution, the PPS output, or UTC accuracy. A timing discrepancy with valid OSNMA is possible without any attack. Put this distinction on the first slide. ([defence-industry-space.ec.europa.eu](https://defence-industry-space.ec.europa.eu/galileos-osnma-authentication-service-now-operational-2025-08-25_en?utm_source=openai))
- **MAJOR — Delayed authentication is not a per-second integrity verdict.** Galileo I/NAV subframes are 30 seconds; authentication depends on receiving the relevant data, tags and subsequently disclosed keys. GSC describes loose GST synchronization requirements of **30–300 seconds**, depending on mode. SDD Annex D’s illustrative **95% time to first fix with authenticated data** for ADKD0 is 130 s hot / 300 s warm / up to six hours cold under its stated conditions; that is not a guarantee for the first tag or for your capture. Show `pending`, `unavailable/stale`, `authenticated for these data`, and `verification rejected` where explicitly supported. Do not convert missing data, startup or an unavailable trust state into a failed tag, or backdate knowledge to the reception epoch. No EO-style spatial resolution or revisit applies; the important cadence is subframes, coverage, reception quality and authentication delay. ([gsc-europa.eu](https://www.gsc-europa.eu/galileo/faq?utm_source=openai))
- **MAJOR — Delete causal labels and the 0–100 “PNT trust” score.** Missing authentication does not diagnose jamming, and a rejected verification does not by itself diagnose forged data. A good tag plus clock disagreement does not establish replay. Track three independent axes: **navigation evidence**, **clock agreement**, **reference health**. For the demo, choose a plainly illustrative limit `L`. With absolute measured offset `d` and declared uncertainty `u`, mark `outside` only when `d − u > L` for three consecutive fresh samples; mark `within` when `d + u ≤ L`; otherwise mark `indeterminate`. Missing/stale samples or an unhealthy reference make the clock result unknown. Preserve the signed offset and threshold in the bundle. Do not market the designs’ 5G/PMU/finance numbers as interchangeable site SLAs. ([gsc-europa.eu](https://www.gsc-europa.eu/sites/default/files/sites/all/files/Galileo-OSNMA-SDD_v1.0.pdf?utm_source=openai))
- **MAJOR — Drop EGNOS/GPS “independent” residuals, HAS, SAS and generic RAIM from the demo.** Current EGNOS Open Service augments GPS L1; it is not a Galileo ranging-authentication service or an independent substation clock. HAS supplies precision corrections, not the proposed authenticated centimetre position. SAS has real 2026 ranging-authentication demonstrations, but that is not a publicly operational weekend dependency. Vendor observables such as C/N₀, AGC and integrity flags can be retained later as context, not used as causal labels. No Copernicus is needed. ([egnos.gsc-europa.eu](https://egnos.gsc-europa.eu/documents/egnos-open-service-sdd?utm_source=openai))
- **MAJOR — No automatic holdover and no EN 16605 conformance claim.** EN 16605:2024 concerns Galileo timing receiver requirements and associated tests. A hybrid software replay does not establish receiver conformance or safe failover. EUSPA’s STARGATE project already targets receiver validation and integration with timing servers for holdover decisions. Rename Wonder’s “conformance scorecard” to **evidence coverage checklist**. The ticket can say “timing engineer: inspect reference and review the approved holdover procedure”; it must never issue the command or declare the oscillator safe. ([webstore.ansi.org](https://webstore.ansi.org/preview-pages/DS/preview_M382651CURES.pdf?utm_source=openai))
