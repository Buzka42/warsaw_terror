### Why the ranking undervalues it

The ranking correctly demolishes the original demo—continuous signed truck tracks and Sentinel-1 identifying shipments—but underestimates the excellent company hiding underneath: **independent GNSS incident evidence for regulated transport**.

The SENT pivot fixes three problems simultaneously:

- **Immediate, quantifiable pain:** a geolocation gap can trigger a PLN 10,000 carrier fine. On 13 May 2026, the Kraków administrative court [quashed such decisions](https://sigil.com.ua/en/guide/gps-signal-gap-court-ruling-2026) because authorities refused to investigate why an apparently functioning app stopped delivering positions. That is documented demand for better evidence, not a hypothetical regulatory pull.
- **The data-owner objection disappears:** for sanctions auditing, the suspect carrier controls the tachograph data. For SENT defence, that same carrier is the paying customer and voluntarily supplies its logs.
- **The external environment is deteriorating:** Polish measurements showed full-scale GNSS disruption on [63% of days during 1–19 August 2026](https://www.euronews.com/2026/08/25/phones-drones-and-ships-running-blind-as-gps-malfunctions-across-poland). Meanwhile, smart tachograph v2 is already mandatory in international transport and [includes Galileo OSNMA](https://dtc.jrc.ec.europa.eu/dtc_smart_tachograph.php.html).

Sigil validates the market for gap alerts and retained histories, but its visible offering leaves room for independent interference corroboration, causal classification and automated evidence packs.

### The winning version

Build **“SENT Black Box”**, not a sanctions-radar dashboard:

1. Ingest sparse tachograph events honestly—journey endpoints, three-hour driving positions, border crossings and loading events—plus fleet-telematics positions, raw receiver health, OSNMA status, cellular connectivity, app logs and SENT acknowledgements.
2. Correlate gaps with independent reference-station or sensor observations. Return **likely receiver interference / likely connectivity or register failure / indeterminate**. Never claim OSNMA detects jamming or proves a route.
3. Alert the dispatcher before the one-hour stop obligation becomes critical, preserve contemporaneous evidence, and generate a timestamped appeal pack containing the route timeline, corroborating observations, inspection checklist and draft Article 22(3) waiver argument.

Demo the documented four-hour Kraków case alongside an August 2026 Pomerania interference episode, including a control gap outside the interference area. Keep sanctions-assurance certificates as the European expansion story: vendor-neutral GNSS incident evidence for carriers, forwarders and cargo insurers. Drop Sentinel-1 unless it answers a separately validated question.

### Single biggest risk

**Causal attribution.** GNSS jamming, mobile backhaul failure, application faults and SENT outages can all produce the same silence. Without independent, contemporaneous measurements, the product cannot prove innocence—it becomes an attractive PDF generator that incumbent telematics platforms can replicate.

*Second Sight is runner-up for its exceptional real-attack narrative and deterministic replay, but substation metering and its inability to restore remote control cap its operational value.*
